## Critical finding: there are two "Araku coffees"

This changes your sourcing plan, so read it before you contact anyone.

**1. ARAKU (SAMTFMACS / Naandi Foundation)** — the premium brand you have seen in Paris and Bangalore. Roughly 10,000 farmers across 520 villages, SCA scores of 85+ with micro-lots above 90, their own central processing unit in the valley, their own roastery and café in Bangalore, their own D2C site.

**They are vertically integrated on purpose.** Their entire brand story is *no intermediaries between farmer and cup*. You are an intermediary. They will not wholesale to you, and their green-bean arm sells to established roasters abroad. **Do not spend a week trying.** Total valley production is only around 100 tonnes a year with roughly half under their own brand — supply is genuinely scarce and they have no reason to give any of it to a first-time buyer.

**2. GCC — Girijan Co-operative Corporation** — a Government of Andhra Pradesh body that procures coffee directly from tribal growers across Araku, Ananthagiri, Paderu, G Madugula, Chintapalli and GK Veedhi.

**They are headquartered in your city.** East Point Colony, Visakhapatnam 530017 · 0891-2796164 / 0891-2581661 · apgirijan1956@gmail.com

They sell 100% pure Araku coffee, a 70:30 chicory blend called Vaishakhi, roasted beans in 500 g and 1 kg packs, and instant coffee. Private resellers already market GCC product under their own labels — which is simultaneously your proof of concept and your competition.

**3. Independent traders and estates.** Araku-region sellers list AA-grade arabica around ₹600/kg on IndiaMart. Farmgate cherry runs near ₹40/kg. These are your margin play and your relationship play, but they are unvetted — samples before money, always.

**Your realistic path is GCC or an independent trader, not the ARAKU brand.**

## Week 1 (10–16 August) — Supply

Walk into GCC in East Point Colony. Do not email first; you are twenty minutes away and a person in the room outranks an inbox. Ask for: wholesale price list, minimum order quantity, whether they sell unbranded or bulk roasted, pack sizes, and lead time.

Then contact three independent Araku-region traders through IndiaMart. Ask each for the same four things: price per kg, grade, roast date, and a sample.

**Roast date is the question most first buyers forget.** Roasted coffee is meaningfully past its best after about a month and those GCC-style products carry roughly six-month shelf lives. Coffee sitting in a warehouse since March is not a product you want your name on.

**Note the season.** The Araku harvest runs roughly November through February. In August you are buying from the last crop, and you will not see a harvest before you leave. That is fine for stock, but it means your "supplier who takes your call from LA" has to be a relationship built on three conversations, not on having worked a season together. Weight that in who you choose.

**Deliverable by 16 August:** three written quotes, samples of each in your hands, one visit to the valley if the monsoon roads permit.

## Week 2 (17–23 August) — Product and price

Taste the samples side by side. Pick one. Buy **5 kg**, not more — roughly ₹4,000–5,000.

Package into 250 g pouches — 20 bags. Valve pouches with your label, roughly ₹20–30 each. Label must carry: FSSAI number, net weight, roast date, ingredients, your address, and a phone number. This is a legal requirement, not a design choice.

**Pricing.** Reseller shelf prices for GCC-branded Araku ground coffee sit near ₹220 for a retail pack; the ARAKU premium brand sells far above that. Your 250 g bag should land at **₹350–450**. Cost per bag at 5 kg is roughly ₹250–275 all-in, so you clear ₹100–175 a bag. That is a real margin and a defensible position between the government commodity pack and the luxury brand.

Ten bags to strangers is roughly ₹4,000 of revenue. This will not make you money. It buys you the only thing you actually need: proof that a person who owes you nothing will pay.

## Week 3 (24–30 August) — Sell. Last full week before the semester.

This is the week that decides the venture, and it is the last one with your full 21 hours.

**Channel one: one physical weekend stall.** A Vizag market, a beach-road weekend spot, or a mall kiosk. Strangers, cash, immediate feedback, and you watch faces as people taste. Brew samples — nobody buys ₹400 coffee from a stranger without tasting it.

**Channel two: an Instagram page plus WhatsApp ordering.** ₹2,000 of local ads geo-targeted to Vizag. This is the asset your sister inherits, so she should be building it with you from day one, not receiving it in September.

Skip corporate gifting and café wholesale for now. Both are slow-cycle and neither produces ten individual buyers in three weeks.

**Track every sale in a spreadsheet:** name, how they found you, what they paid, whether they are family or jamaat. The last column is the honest one.

## Things that will bite you

**US tax.** You are almost certainly a US person for tax purposes. Worldwide income is reportable on your US return regardless of where it was earned or whether it stayed in India. If Indian accounts ever aggregate above $10,000 at any point in a year, FBAR reporting applies — you are far below that, but know where the line is. Business income also surfaces on your 2028–29 FAFSA, which is why clean books from day one are not optional.

**GST.** Registration is generally not required for goods below a ₹40 lakh annual turnover threshold in Andhra Pradesh, but it *is* required to sell on marketplaces like Amazon or Flipkart regardless of size. Verify the current threshold before you assume. You are nowhere near it — this only matters if you go the marketplace route.

**Monsoon.** August in the Eastern Ghats. Valley roads to Araku can be bad. Plan the trip with a fallback day.

**The handover is the real risk, not the coffee.** Your sister is inheriting a business she did not choose, run by remote instruction, while you are in a different hemisphere getting married and applying to six universities. Build everything — the label, the Instagram, the supplier relationship, the customer list — so that it is *hers* by 15 September, not so that it is yours with her holding the keys.
